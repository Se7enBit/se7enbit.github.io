Privacy policy

Flamingo – Hands-Free Lists for Android · Last updated 23 October 2026

Flamingo does not collect, store on a server, sell or share any personal data. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The app does not have permission to access the internet.

Your lists and photos

Lists, list photos, doodles and settings are stored only on your phone, in the app's private storage. Uninstalling the app deletes them. If Android's backup to your Google account is turned on, the system may include this data in that backup. Google handles that backup under its own privacy policy, and the developer has no access to it.

Microphone

The microphone is used only if you turn on voice commands, and only while a hands-free session is running. Speech is recognised offline on your phone, and only a fixed set of command words ("next", "done", "stop" and a few others) is matched. Audio is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere. Microphone access is requested at the moment you turn voice commands on, and you can turn it off at any time in the app or in Android's settings.

Camera and photo library

To create a photo list, you can take a picture with your camera app or pick an image with Android's photo picker. Flamingo only receives the one photo you chose, and stores a copy of it on your phone.

Sharing

When you share a list as a link, the list's title and items are encoded into the part of the link after the #. You decide where to send it. Browsers do not send that part of a link to any server. If the link is opened in a browser without the app, a web page shows the list by decoding it in the browser. Only in browsers too old to decode it does the page load a small open-source library (pako) from the jsDelivr CDN. Like any web request, that one makes your IP address visible to jsDelivr.

Exported PDFs are created on your phone and go only to the app you choose in the share sheet.

Children

Flamingo is a general-purpose list app and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone.
